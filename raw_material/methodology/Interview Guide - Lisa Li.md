# Interview Guide - Lisa Li

Interview Guide- from my personal reflections
1. The Warm-Up: purpose: to connect with the interviewer.
* Start with "Small Talk": Use easy topics to break the ice (e.g., the weather, weekend activities, or personal interests).
* Keep it Professional: avoid politics or controversial "hot topics."
2. The Introduction
* Be Selective: Don’t just read your entire resume. Focus on 3–4 high-impact activities or projects that you are most proud of.
* Highlight Results: When describing your work, explain your specific contribution and the data/results you achieved.
* Show Passion: Let your enthusiasm for your projects shine through!
3. The Coding Session
* Think Out Loud: Don't code in silence. Explain your logic so the interviewer can follow your thought process.
* Clarify First: Ask questions before you start writing code. Make sure you understand the success criteria (e.g., "What does a 'pass' look like for this problem?").
4. Domain Knowledge
* Ask Before Answering: ask for clarification to ensure you’re on the right track.
* Watch the Room: If you notice the interviewer trying to interrupt, stop and listen. They might be trying to guide you.
* Reframe if Needed: If a question is confusing, try to rephrase it back to them to confirm your understanding.
* The "Honesty" Rule: If you don't know an answer, don't fake it. Say: "I haven't had the chance to go deep into that area yet, but it's something I plan to study before I join."
* Follow-Up: At the very end, ask: "Does that answer your question?" to ensure you’ve fully addressed their concerns.
5. Closing Strong:  when interviewer ask do you have any questions? 
* Ask Tailored Questions: Avoid "generic" questions. Instead, use this time to show genuine interest.
    * Bad Example: "What does a typical day look like for this team?"
    * Good Example: "What personality traits help someone succeed in this specific office/culture?" ( My answer: curiosity, proactive, assertive, eager to learn) 
    Use this part to show your strong interest and ready to join. 

It’s about "Team Fit" over "Expertise": You don't need to be the smartest person in the room—you need to be the most collaborative. Show them you are easy to work with, open to feedback, and excited to learn.  Be assertive/professional while easy to work with.
