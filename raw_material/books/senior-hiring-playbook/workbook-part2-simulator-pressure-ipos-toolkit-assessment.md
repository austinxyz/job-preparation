# Workbook Part 2: Practice & Preparation Tools

## Section 6: Stress Test Simulator

### Practice Protocol
1. Select a question from the bank below
2. Set a timer for 90 seconds
3. Record yourself answering
4. Review using the self-scoring checklist
5. Log practice in the tracker

### Question Bank by Stress Test (30 questions)

**Decision-Making (5)**
1. Describe a controversial decision you made that others disagreed with.
2. Tell me about a time you chose speed over quality.
3. Walk me through a decision you made with incomplete data.
4. Describe a decision you made that you later realized was wrong.
5. How do you decide what NOT to do?

**Judgment & Tradeoff (5)**
6. How do you balance technical debt against feature development?
7. Tell me about a time you had to choose between two good options.
8. How do you prioritize when everything seems important?
9. Describe a situation where you had to say no to something valuable.
10. How do you think about build versus buy decisions?

**Narrative Compression (5)**
11. Tell me about your career in 90 seconds.
12. Summarize your biggest accomplishment in 60 seconds.
13. What's the #1 leadership lesson you've learned?
14. Describe your leadership philosophy in three sentences.
15. Why are you looking for a new role?

**Cross-Functional (5)**
16. Describe your hardest cross-organizational conflict.
17. How did you align leaders who disagreed on priorities?
18. Tell me about a time you influenced without authority.
19. How do you handle a peer who isn't meeting commitments?
20. Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager.

**Scope & Strategic (5)**
21. What's your biggest accomplishment?
22. What impact have you had in your current role?
23. How have you made your organization better?
24. How do you think about platform investment?
25. What's your approach to building high-performing teams?

**Emotional Regulation (5)**
26. Tell me about your biggest failure.
27. Describe a time you received critical feedback.
28. Tell me about a time you missed a major deadline.
29. How do you handle underperformance on your team?
30. Tell me about a time you had to deliver bad news to leadership.

### Self-Scoring Checklist (8 criteria per answer)

| Criteria | Yes | No |
|----------|-----|-----|
| Did I lead with a decision or conclusion? | ☐ | ☐ |
| Did I articulate at least one tradeoff? | ☐ | ☐ |
| Did I state a principle that guided me? | ☐ | ☐ |
| Did I connect to business impact? | ☐ | ☐ |
| Was my answer under 90 seconds? | ☐ | ☐ |
| Did I avoid "we" when I should have said "I"? | ☐ | ☐ |
| Did I maintain emotional neutrality? | ☐ | ☐ |
| Did I frame at appropriate scope for target role? | ☐ | ☐ |

**Scoring:** 7-8 Yes = Strong | 5-6 Yes = Adequate | <5 Yes = Needs work

## Section 7: Pressure Inoculation Tracker (8 weeks)

Track every practice session:

### Weeks 1-2: Foundation
Focus: Decision-first framing, principle articulation
Daily: 1 answer, 90-second max, record yourself
Track: Date | Question Practiced | Time | Score/8

### Weeks 3-4: Time Pressure
Focus: Getting to the point faster, cutting unnecessary context
Daily: 2 answers with 60-second limit, random question selection
Track: Date | Questions Practiced | Under 60s? | Score/8

### Weeks 5-6: Challenge Pressure
Focus: Maintaining composure under challenge
Daily: Practice with a partner who pushes back, interrupts, expresses skepticism
Track: Date | Partner/Challenge Type | Stayed Calm? | Notes

### Weeks 7-8: Full Simulation
Focus: Full interview performance under realistic conditions
Weekly: Full mock interview (45-60 min), video call, business attire, no notes
Track: Mock# | Date | What Went Well | Focus for Next Time

### Week 8 Progress Indicators (all should be checked)
- [ ] Can answer any question in 90 seconds without feeling rushed
- [ ] Pushback makes me curious rather than defensive
- [ ] Naturally frame answers as decisions rather than activities
- [ ] Can discuss failures without emotional charge
- [ ] Voice stays steady under challenge
- [ ] Can maintain executive-level framing throughout a full interview

## Section 9: Interview Prep Toolkit (per loop)

Complete 1-2 weeks before each interview loop.

### 9.1 Interviewer Profiles
For each interviewer: Name/Role | Background | Likely Focus | Stories to Use

### 9.2 Anticipated Challenges
What concerns might they have? Prepare RAPID responses:

| Likely Concern/Challenge | My RAPID Response |
|--------------------------|-------------------|

### 9.3 Questions to Ask
3-5 strategic questions that demonstrate genuine engagement:
1. [Hiring Manager] "What does success look like at 6 months? At 18 months?"
2. [Hiring Manager] "What's the hardest part of this role not obvious from the JD?"
3. [Executive] "What's the biggest strategic challenge facing engineering right now?"
4. [Peer] "How do decisions get made when your function and this one disagree?"
5. [Anyone] "What would you want the person in this role to understand about your team's priorities?"

### 9.4 Pre-Interview Checklist (48-72 Hours Before)

**Preparation:**
- [ ] Completed full mock interview
- [ ] Reviewed active story rotation (5-7 stories)
- [ ] Reviewed RAPID responses for anticipated challenges
- [ ] Practiced Executive Narrative (timed — 75-90 seconds)
- [ ] Reviewed culture calibration notes for this company

**Logistics:**
- [ ] Confirmed interview times and format
- [ ] Tested video/audio technology
- [ ] Prepared environment (quiet, good lighting, professional background)
- [ ] Outfit selected

**Physical/Mental:**
- [ ] Good sleep planned
- [ ] Exercise scheduled
- [ ] Stopped active preparation (avoid over-cramming — confidence comes from work already done)

## Key Meta-Insight from the Workbook

The workbook's value is in **converting knowledge into muscle memory through repetition and tracking**. The self-scoring checklist (8 criteria) is the most immediately actionable tool — it gives you a consistent measurement framework so you can track real improvement across practice sessions, not just subjective sense of "getting better."

The Pressure Inoculation Tracker is the highest-leverage tool — most senior candidates skip it because 8 weeks feels like a lot. But pressure stability is not built through knowledge; it's built through repeated exposure to challenge conditions. The tracker enforces the discipline.
