# Part 6: Failure Modes, Preparation & Templates

## Common Failure Modes by Level

### Staff/Principal → Senior Staff/Director

**Core challenge:** Moving from technical excellence to organizational impact.

| Failure Mode | What it sounds like | The Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Technical Identity Lock | "My biggest accomplishment was designing our event-driven architecture. We achieved 99.99% uptime." | Reframe technical work as business decisions: "The strategic question was whether our architecture could support 10x growth without 10x infrastructure cost." |
| Missing Influence Evidence | "I influenced the team to adopt TypeScript by building a proof of concept." | Prepare explicit influence stories with business case + org alignment + multi-team execution. |
| Scope Ceiling | Impact described at team or project level | Use Scope Ladder deliberately; upgrade key stories to system-level before interview. |

### Senior Manager → Director

**Core challenge:** Moving from team optimization to multi-team strategy.

| Failure Mode | What it sounds like | The Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Team-Centric Framing | "I'm proud of the team culture I've built. High retention, great velocity." | Reframe around org contribution: "I identified that engineering culture varied across teams, creating cross-team friction. I established practices adopted across three teams — not by mandate, but by demonstrating results." |
| Process Over Judgment | "We handle technical debt through a quarterly review process." | Show judgment behind the process: "The process surfaces candidates; my judgment determines priority. I evaluate debt on three criteria: velocity impact, risk concentration, and optionality cost." |
| Missing Strategic Connection | Excellent execution stories without connecting to company strategy | Every accomplishment story: add a sentence connecting to business strategy ("This project mattered because..." / "The strategic context was..."). |

### Director → VP

**Core challenge:** Moving from functional leadership to executive partnership.

| Failure Mode | What it sounds like | The Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Functional Silo | "I've built a strong platform organization — 50 engineers, clear roadmap, high delivery velocity." | Frame as business contribution: "I transformed platform from a cost center to a strategic capability. Revenue-per-engineer increased 30% over two years, partly attributable to platform investment." |
| Manager of Managers Identity | "I oversee four teams with six managers. I focus on developing my managers." | Lead with business accountability: "I'm accountable for platform reliability and developer productivity. The managers and teams are how I deliver on that accountability, but my job is the outcome, not the structure." |
| Insufficient Executive Presence | Communication style of a senior manager, not a VP | Practice, not just awareness. Record yourself. Compare to executives you admire. Pay attention to pace, pauses, confidence without arrogance, narrative authority. |

**Failure Modes Summary:**

| Transition | Common Failure | Core Fix |
|------------|---------------|----------|
| Staff → Director | Technical identity lock; missing influence | Reframe technical work as business decisions |
| Sr Manager → Director | Team-centric framing; process over judgment | Show judgment and cross-team impact |
| Director → VP | Functional silo; manager-of-managers identity | Lead with business accountability and executive presence |

## End-to-End Preparation Playbook

### Phase 1: Foundation (Before Any Interviews)

**Step 1: Build your story library**
Document 10-15 stories covering: major decisions, conflicts, failures, cross-functional initiatives, org change, technical leadership, team building. For each:
- One-sentence summary
- Key decision made
- Tradeoffs navigated
- Principle applied
- Business impact
- Key learning

**Step 2: Develop your Executive Narrative**
Write and memorize your 90-second leadership story. Practice until natural, not rehearsed.

**Step 3: Identify your failure modes**
Review the failure modes for your current level. Be honest about which apply. Build targeted practice.

**Step 4: Start pressure inoculation**
Begin the 8-week protocol. Skills compound.

### Phase 2: Company-Specific (1-2 weeks before interview loop)

**Step 1: Culture research** (five dimensions)
Sources: Glassdoor patterns, LinkedIn executive backgrounds, company blog/public communications, news, network contacts.

**Step 2: Interviewer research**
For each interviewer: role, background, likely evaluation focus, which of your stories will resonate.

**Step 3: Story selection**
From library, select 5-7 stories most relevant to this company. Practice until smooth.

**Step 4: Anticipate challenges**
Gaps in experience? Short tenure? A visible failure? Prepare RAPID responses.

### Phase 3: Final Preparation (48-72 hours before)

**Step 1:** Full mock interview — video call, business attire, no notes, 45-60 minutes, specific feedback.

**Step 2:** Prepare 3-5 thoughtful questions that demonstrate strategic thinking and genuine interest.

**Step 3:** Confirm logistics. Test technology. Get good sleep. Exercise.

**Step 4:** Review Executive Narrative, active story rotation, RAPID responses. Then stop preparing. Over-preparation creates anxiety.

## Scripts & Templates

### Script 1: Executive Narrative Template

```
[Identity Statement]
"I'm a [type of leader] who [distinctive approach]. My focus is [core problem you solve]."

[Scope Marker]
"Currently, I [role description] at [company], where I [scope: team size, budget, impact area]."

[Operating Principles]
"I believe [first principle]. I've found that [second principle]. My approach is [how you operationalize]."

[Signature Lesson]
"The most important thing I've learned is [key insight]. This came from [brief context]
and has shaped [how you lead now]."

[Current Focus]
"Right now, I'm focused on [current priority]. That's what drew me to this conversation — 
[connection to the role/company]."
```

### Script 2: Decision Story Template

```
"The decision I made was [clear statement]."
"This mattered because [stakes/context]."
"The tradeoff was between [A] and [B]. [A] would have [benefit] but [cost]. 
[B] would have [different benefit] but [different cost]."
"I chose [choice] because [principle or reasoning]."
"The outcome was [result]. What I learned was [insight]."
```

### Script 3: Failure Story Template

```
"The failure was [clear statement of what went wrong]."
"My role in it was [honest ownership]."
"At the time, I believed [original reasoning]. Looking back, I missed [what you didn't see]."
"The impact was [consequence]."
"What I took from it was [specific lesson]. Since then, I [how you've applied the lesson]."
```

### Script 4: Conflict Story Template

```
"The conflict was between [parties] about [topic]."
"[Party A]'s concern was [legitimate interest]. [Party B]'s concern was [different legitimate interest]. Both were valid."
"My approach was [how you handled it]. The principle guiding me was [principle]."
"We resolved it by [resolution]."
"The relationship [outcome]. What I learned was [insight]."
```

### Script 5: Challenge Response (RAPID)

```
"[Recognize] That's a fair question. / You're right to ask about that."
"[Anchor] My perspective is grounded in [principle or framework]."
"[Provide] Specifically, [concrete example or data point]."
"[Implications] What this means is [consequence or learning]."
"[Direct] I'm happy to go deeper on [expand], or we could discuss [pivot]."
```

## Strategic Questions to Ask Interviewers

**For Hiring Managers:**
- "What does success look like in this role at 6 months? At 18 months?"
- "What's the hardest part of this role that isn't obvious from the job description?"
- "What's the most important decision this role will need to make in the next year?"
- "How does this role fit into the broader organizational strategy?"

**For Peer Interviewers:**
- "What's your experience been partnering with this function? What works well? What's challenging?"
- "How do decisions get made when your function and this one disagree?"
- "What would you want the person in this role to understand about your team's priorities?"

**For Executive Interviewers:**
- "What's the biggest strategic challenge facing engineering/product right now?"
- "How do you think about the balance between [relevant tradeoff for the company]?"
- "What do you wish engineering/product did differently?"
- "What excites you most about where the company is headed?"

## Closing Insight

The leaders who consistently land senior roles aren't necessarily smarter or more experienced than those who struggle. They've learned to communicate their experience in patterns that match how executive panels evaluate.

The gap between knowing this material and executing under pressure is bridged only through **deliberate practice**. The preparation investment is worth it — a single loop can change career trajectory, compensation, challenges, and growth.

You've done the hard work of building experience worth talking about. Now learn to talk about it in ways that land.
