# eBay - AI-Augmented Engineering Management

## Context

As eBay expanded AI tool adoption across the engineering org — ChatGPT, Claude Code, Glean, NotebookLM — engineering managers faced a real choice: use AI to produce polished documents, or use it to reclaim time and invest it in higher-leverage people work. The admin layer of EM work — status updates, resume screening, performance reviews, quarterly planning, stakeholder communication — was consuming a disproportionate share of time without creating compounding value. I decided to systematically rebuild my own management workflows around AI tooling across six areas, with one consistent constraint: preserve human judgment where it matters, and use AI only where the task is high-frequency and low-judgment.

*Note: The team-wide AI adoption story — MCP server for triage, spec-driven development, hiring skills rollout — is documented separately in [[experience/eBay - AI Innovation]]. This story covers the personal EM workflow improvements.*

## Actions

- **Upward management:** I built a workflow to transform raw meeting notes into executive summaries emphasizing outcomes over implementation detail. QBR preparation dropped from a full day to a few hours; weekly status updates from 1–2 hours to 15 minutes.
- **Performance reviews:** I used NotebookLM and Glean to organize 1-on-1 notes and career framework history across the year, enabling evidence-based reviews instead of memory-dependent assessments. Engineers reported feeling genuinely seen in reviews — the qualitative signal that the process worked.
- **Hiring pipeline:** I built structured AI skills for resume screening, interview question generation, and feedback aggregation. Interview overhead dropped from 3–4 hours per interview to ~90 minutes; hiring cycle compressed from 3+ months to 4–6 weeks.
- **Project management and OKR planning:** I shifted quarterly planning from debate-heavy sessions to 30-minute alignment checks backed by AI-generated transparent documentation. OKR completion improved from ~50% to ~80%.
- **Remote onboarding:** I built structured documentation that reduced new-hire context requests; time-to-productivity for new engineers dropped from 3 months to 6 weeks.
- **Judgment hygiene throughout:** I deliberately formed my own opinion before consulting AI on any decision — treating it as a thinking partner to stress-test reasoning, not a source of conclusions. I stayed focused on depth in 2–3 core workflows rather than broad tool adoption, because optional tools get used sometimes and structural tools get used always.

## Results

- Admin layer of EM work reduced to roughly one-third of previous time — time reinvested in 1-on-1s, technical context-building, and cross-team coordination.
- Hiring: 8+ engineers hired across Europe and India within 3 months; pipeline of 200–300 candidates managed without adding hiring coordinator headcount.
- OKR completion: ~50% → ~80%.
- New-hire ramp: 3 months → 6 weeks.
- Hiring skills and onboarding agents adopted by other teams across the org — built for my team's needs, generalized to broader use.

## Learnings

- The most durable gains came from structural integration, not optional tooling. A workflow that runs automatically every time (notes → executive summary) compounds; a tool you remember to use when you have time doesn't.
- Performance reviews improved most from the year-round discipline — organizing 1-on-1 notes continuously rather than reconstructing them at review time. AI helped surface patterns across months of notes; the judgment about what those patterns meant remained mine.
- Forming my own opinion before consulting AI was the single most important practice for maintaining judgment quality. The failure mode I was watching for: outsourcing decisions to a tool and losing the ability to evaluate the output. Using AI to challenge a conclusion I'd already formed is very different from using it to form the conclusion.

## Signal Areas

**Primary:** Growth (systematically rebuilt personal management workflows to create compounding leverage; developed a principled approach to where AI augments vs. where human judgment must remain; adopted new capabilities before asking the team to change how they worked)

**Secondary:** Ownership (self-initiated across six EM workflows — no one asked for this; piloted on real work before scaling; held to a consistent principle throughout)

## Key Questions

**Q: How do you decide which parts of your EM workflow to automate versus keep human?**
Talking points: High-frequency, low-judgment tasks are the target — status summaries, resume filtering, OKR drafts, structured feedback aggregation. Human judgment stays where trust and nuance matter most: 1-on-1 conversations, performance calibration, final hiring decisions. The test: would getting this wrong damage a relationship or misrepresent someone? If yes, keep the human in the loop.

**Q: Tell me about a time you significantly improved your hiring process.**
Talking points: Name the bottleneck (3–4 hours per interview, 3+ month cycle). Describe the specific changes (AI skills for screening, question generation, feedback aggregation). Quantify the result (90-minute interviews, 4–6 week cycle, 8+ hires across two regions in 3 months). Note that the hiring skills were later adopted by other teams — built for the immediate need, designed to be reusable.

**Q: How do you run effective performance reviews at scale?**
Talking points: Avoid memory-dependent assessments by organizing evidence year-round, not at review time. Use structured 1-on-1 notes and career framework tracking throughout the year; AI helps surface patterns across months of data, but the judgment about what those patterns mean stays human. Engineers feeling genuinely seen is the qualitative signal the process worked.

**Q: How do you think about AI augmentation vs. AI replacement in management work?**
Talking points: Form your own opinion first, then use AI to stress-test it — not the reverse. The failure mode is outsourcing decisions to a tool and losing the ability to evaluate the output. AI that compresses the time cost of low-judgment tasks is high-value; AI that replaces the judgment itself is a liability. The distinction matters more as the stakes of the decision go up.

## Summary

As AI tools proliferated across eBay's engineering org, I chose to systematically rebuild my own management workflows rather than use AI ad hoc. The insight driving it: AI should compress the time cost of low-judgment tasks so the recovered time can go into higher-leverage people work — not replace judgment itself. Across six EM workflows (upward management, performance reviews, hiring, OKR planning, onboarding, and incident response), targeted AI-assisted processes produced measurable improvements: hiring cycle from 3+ months to 4–6 weeks, OKR completion from 50% to 80%, new-hire ramp from 3 months to 6 weeks.

The consistent practice throughout was forming my own opinion before consulting AI — using it as a thinking partner to stress-test conclusions, not as a source of them. That discipline is what made the adoption durable: the tooling accelerated judgment without eroding it. The hiring skills and onboarding agents built for my team were later adopted by other teams across the org, which validated the design principle — build for the immediate need in a way that generalizes.
